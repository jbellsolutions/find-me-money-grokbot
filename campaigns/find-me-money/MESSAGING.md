**Until that document is in the workspace, the agents draft from this outline only. No piece that uses a proof point, a count, a screenshot or a line about the industry's conduct is drafted until the document is in and Justin has cleared that claim on its list.**

## The position

Find Me Money is the business-funding company that tells you how the game works. Most of the industry wants your application. This one wants you to understand what happens to it: who gets paid, what the offer in front of you really costs, and what you can say to get a better one. Education comes first; the application is the last step.

## What it stands for

1. Show how everyone gets paid, including us.
2. Start with what the money is for, not with a product.
3. Teach before taking an application.
4. Your information goes where you say.
5. Say so when the answer is "don't borrow".
6. Everything claimed can be checked.

Each is a promise the business has to keep before it is said in public. The private document lists what has to be true first.

## The eight education pillars

| # | Pillar | What the viewer learns |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | How they get paid | Buy rate and sell rate, points, factor rates, fees |
| 2 | How to talk an offer down | The questions to ask: the buy rate, the total payback, every fee, the APR |
| 3 | Brokers and "direct lenders" | Who is really funding, and how to compare offers without hurting the file |
| 4 | The products and when to use each | One page per product: what it is, what it costs, who qualifies, when it fits, when to walk away |
| 5 | Why you were denied and what fixes it | What each kind of lender screens on |
| 6 | Personal for business, business for personal | When each route is cheaper and what it puts at risk |
| 7 | Holding companies and structure | Needs its own research before any content |
| 8 | Get any project funded | Start from the project, match the product, mind the order |

Pillars 6 and 8 are the "creative alternative ways". They carry the most risk: every home-equity message says in the same breath that the home is the collateral, and pillars 6 and 7 wait on a licensed review before content (the private document, claim 8).

## Content types

- **Reactions:** a real pitch on screen, with the sender covered, and what it is actually saying.
- **Talkbacks:** an answer to one of the industry's own lines.
- **Breakdowns:** one mechanism taught in under a minute.
- **UGC:** owners showing their own inbox, texts or story, with their permission each time.
- **Story:** Justin's own, serialised. Drafted only from the private document.

## Voice

- One person talking to a phone camera. First person, present tense.
- The receipt on screen in the first three seconds.
- Plain numbers.
- Angry at the system, respectful of the viewer.
- Every piece teaches one thing the viewer can use that day.

It never: names a company as a wrongdoer without a court finding or regulator action to point at; promises approval, an amount, a rate or a timeline; says "no risk"; teaches anything that amounts to misstating facts on a credit application; shows someone's name, face, number or message unredacted.

Words it avoids: guaranteed, pre-approved, instant, secret, loophole, hack, "bad credit OK", "no doc".
