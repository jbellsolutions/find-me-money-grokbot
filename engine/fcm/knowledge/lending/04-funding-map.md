# The funding map

When someone tells us what it's for and roughly how much, they get the options we'd look at first, and the ones we'd skip. Triage drafts it from the same rows in
`config/sales.json`, and you can improve it from this page. Say "may" throughout. A partner reviews and the lender decides. Never quote a rate, a term or an approval.
These are starting points, not offers.

| They need | Look at | Usually skip | Worth saying |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash for slow months, payroll | Line of credit, term loan | Revenue-based, unless it's urgent | Fast money is usually the most expensive money |
| A one-time project or expansion | Term loan, SBA if they can wait | Revenue-based | SBA is often the longest and the slowest |
| A truck, machine or gear | Equipment financing, the vendor's own financing to compare | Cash, if it leaves them short | A partner will likely want the vendor's quote. Don't ask for it by email |
| Money tied up in equipment, trucks or invoices | Equipment-backed financing, invoice factoring | Revenue-based, unless it's urgent | What it's worth counts, and cash flow and credit still matter. Only offer this once a partner does it |
| Customers who pay slowly | Invoice factoring, line of credit | Revenue-based | Works best with solid customers |
| Stuck in daily payments | Debt consolidation or refinance | Another stacked advance | Say nothing about savings. A partner has to look |
| Buying or fixing property to rent or flip | Investor real estate, through a licensed partner | Business credit products | Home-secured loans go to a licensed partner |
| A new company, no revenue yet | Business credit building, 0% credit lines | Term loans, SBA | Some owners may qualify for 0% credit lines after preparation. It takes good personal credit, and a partner decides |
| Bad credit, needs work first | Prep with a credit partner, by referral | Applying now | Never say we remove accurate items |

What lenders tend to ask for: time in business, monthly deposits, credit, what's already owed. Never name a lender.

Never say "business credit cards": it's "0% credit lines". Keep it short, then ask if they'd like an intro. It's theirs either way.
